Otter.ai privacy

Is Otter.ai safe?

Otter.ai encrypts stored data and has a SOC 2 Type 2 report. Two more facts matter for Otter.ai privacy. Otter trains its own AI on de-identified recordings and on transcripts. And the Otter.ai lawsuit, which alleges recording without consent, continues in a US federal court. The court has not decided whether Otter broke the law.

Checked on 10 November 2026 · Every quote comes from Otter's own pages or from the court order

The Otter.ai lawsuit

On 15 August 2025, Justin Brewer filed a proposed class action against Otter.ai in the US District Court for the Northern District of California. NPR reported the filing that day. The case now has seven plaintiffs and the name In re Otter.ai Privacy Litigation, case 25-cv-06911-EKL.

The plaintiffs come from California, Illinois, and Washington. They say the Otter Notetaker recorded their virtual meetings without their consent. In the court's summary of the complaint, the Notetaker joins meetings as a "silent participant" and records "without obtaining the consent of all participants". The plaintiffs also allege that Otter keeps the conversations and voiceprints "for its own commercial use, including to train its automatic speech recognition and machine-learning models".

Otter asked the court to dismiss the whole complaint. In the court's words, Otter argued that the Notetaker "joins meetings as an invited participant and functions as the meeting host's recording tool".

On 13 August 2026, Judge Eumi K. Lee granted that request in part and denied it in part. Claims under the federal Electronic Communications Privacy Act, the California Invasion of Privacy Act (sections 631 and 632), and the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act go forward. So do the claims for unjust enrichment, the claim under California's Unfair Competition Law, the claim for a declaratory judgment, and one plaintiff's privacy claims under California law.

The court dismissed some claims, among them the claims under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act and the Washington Privacy Act, and let the plaintiffs amend those. The plaintiffs withdrew several other claims, and the court dismissed them.

This order is not a verdict. It decides only whether the plaintiffs may sue and whether the complaint says enough for the case to continue. The court called it "its first ruling on the legal sufficiency of the complaint". It has not decided whether Otter broke the law.

Source: the court's order of 13 August 2026 on govinfo.gov. Checked on 10 November 2026. This page reports that order and is not legal advice.

Why Otter joins your meetings, and how to stop it

Otter's help center says the Notetaker "automatically joins your Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams meetings to record, transcribe, and capture your meetings in real time". It joins the calendar events of a person who connected a calendar to Otter. That person can be you or another guest.

The Notetaker does not hide. Otter says it "cannot join a meeting anonymously or without appearing to other participants", and that "it posts a message in the meeting chat with a link to the live transcript". The Otter user can change that chat setting. Otter can also email every guest before the meeting, when that setting is on.

To stop your own Otter from joining:

  1. In Otter, open the Meetings tab.
  2. Click AI Notetaker settings.
  3. Select "Meetings I manually select".

Events that you switched on or off by hand keep their setting, so check your upcoming events afterwards.

To remove Otter from a call that has started, type "stop otter" in the meeting chat. Otter's help center says: "Any meeting participant, including those without an Otter account, can remove Notetaker." Your settings do not control another person's Otter. Ask that person to change the setting.

A note taker that sends nothing

Minute is a free, open source note taker for Mac. It is an app on your computer, not a cloud service.

Your meetings stay on your Mac.

Recording, transcription, and summaries run on your Mac and do not use the network. Minute goes online only to download a model, to list models, and to check for an update.

No bot joins the call.

Minute records your microphone and, on macOS 13 or later, the call's system audio. Nobody on the call sees a new participant. Otter's desktop app can also record without a bot. With Minute, the transcript and the summary are also made on your Mac.

You still have to tell people.

Because nobody sees Minute in the call, say that you are recording. Recording laws differ by place, so check the law where you and the other people are.

Minute has limits. It runs only on a Mac, it has no file import, and it has no audit report like SOC 2. It has no account and no server, and you can read the code.

Questions about Otter.ai

Does Otter.ai sell your data?

Otter's security page says "We do not sell your data." Its privacy policy says that Otter discloses device, cookie, and location information to advertising and analytics providers, and that some of these disclosures "may broadly be considered a "sale" of Personal Information" under US state privacy laws.

Is Otter.ai being sued?

Yes. A proposed class action, In re Otter.ai Privacy Litigation, is in the US District Court for the Northern District of California. On 13 August 2026 the court let the wiretap claims, the biometric privacy claims, and several other claims continue, and it dismissed some claims. The court has not decided whether Otter broke the law.

Does Otter.ai train its AI on my meetings?

Otter's privacy policy lists "training our proprietary AI technology on de-identified audio recordings and on transcriptions (which may contain Personal Information)". Otter says the companies that provide its AI services do not train their models on customer data.

Why is Otter.ai joining my meetings?

Usually, someone on the invite connected a calendar to Otter and has auto-join on, or added the Notetaker to that meeting. If that is you, set auto-join to "Meetings I manually select". During a call, anyone can type "stop otter" in the meeting chat to remove it.

Are AI note takers illegal?

Recording laws apply to them, and those laws differ by place. Otter's help center says that some places require only that the person who records knows about it, and that others, such as California, Illinois, and Florida, require every participant to be notified and to give permission. Tell people before you record. This is not legal advice.
