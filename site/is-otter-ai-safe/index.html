Minute vs Otter: privacy

Is Otter.ai safe? A privacy comparison with Minute

Otter.ai is a cloud service. It encrypts stored data, has a SOC 2 Type 2 report, and trains its own AI on de-identified recordings and on transcripts. Minute is a Mac app that keeps the recording, the transcript, and the summary on your computer. This page compares Otter.ai privacy with Minute, row by row.

Protected in the cloud, or never sent

Otter protects your meetings after it receives them. It encrypts stored data, and an independent auditor reports on its controls. You rely on those controls and on Otter's policy, which allows Otter to train its own AI on de-identified recordings and on transcripts.

Minute never receives your meetings. Recording, transcription, and summaries run on your Mac and do not use the network. Minute has no account and no server. You rely on your own Mac, and you can read the code.

Each choice has a cost. With Otter, your meetings are on another company's servers. With Minute, the notes are plain files with no encryption of their own, so the security of your Mac is your job.

When Otter is the better choice

Minute is not the safer choice for everyone. Otter fits better in three cases.

Your company needs an audited vendor.

Otter has a SOC 2 Type 2 report. Minute has no audit report. It is an open source app, not a vendor with certified controls.

You want everyone to see the note taker.

The Otter Notetaker is visible in the meeting and posts a message in the chat. Nobody sees Minute, so telling people is up to you.

You do not use a Mac.

Minute runs only on macOS 11 or later. Apple silicon is the tested target, and the Intel build is not yet tested on real Intel hardware.

Questions about Otter.ai and Minute

Is Otter.ai safe?

Otter encrypts stored data and has a SOC 2 Type 2 report. It also trains its own AI on de-identified recordings and on transcripts. If your meetings must not leave your computer, a cloud service does not fit, and an app that works offline does.

Does Otter.ai train its AI on my meetings?

Otter's privacy policy lists "training our proprietary AI technology on de-identified audio recordings and on transcriptions (which may contain Personal Information)". Otter says the companies that provide its AI services do not train their models on customer data. Minute has no server, so it receives nothing to train on.

Does Otter.ai sell your data?

Otter's security page says "We do not sell your data." Its privacy policy says that Otter discloses device, cookie, and location information to advertising and analytics providers, and that some of these disclosures "may broadly be considered a "sale" of Personal Information" under US state privacy laws.

Why is Otter.ai joining my meetings?

Usually, someone on the invite connected a calendar to Otter and has auto-join on, or added the Notetaker to that meeting. If that is you, set auto-join to "Meetings I manually select" in Otter's AI Notetaker settings. During a call, anyone can type "stop otter" in the meeting chat to remove it. Minute has no bot, so it never joins a call.

Is Minute safe?

Minute sends nothing from your meetings over the network, and it has no account and no analytics. It also has limits. Notes are plain files with no encryption of their own, and Minute has no audit report. Turn on FileVault, and read the code on GitHub if you want to check.

Is Otter.ai free?

Otter has a free Basic plan with 300 transcription minutes a month and 30 minutes at most per conversation. Minute is free with no quota. See how they compare on price and features on the Otter alternative page.
